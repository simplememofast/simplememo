Glossary — Thinking Tools

What is a Mind Map?

A Mind Map is a visual thinking tool developed by Tony Buzan in the 1970s. Place a central theme in the middle and radiate keywords and ideas outward as branches. It leverages the brain's natural association process, letting you see the entire landscape of a topic at a glance.

By AI Ataka

Definition

Mind Map is a visual thinking method that radiates keywords from a central theme. Created by Tony Buzan, its core rules are: one keyword per branch, use of colors and images, and curved organic branches. Used for brainstorming, planning, study notes, and presentations, it reveals connections between ideas that linear lists cannot show.

How to Create a Mind Map

1. Set the Central Theme
Write your main topic in the center. Adding an image or icon strengthens the visual anchor.
2. Draw Main Branches
Extend main categories as thick curved lines from the center. Use different colors for each branch for intuitive distinction.
3. Add Sub-branches
Extend thinner branches from each main branch for sub-topics. One keyword per branch is ideal.
4. Connect Related Ideas
Draw dotted lines between related items on different branches. You may discover unexpected connections.

FAQ: Mind Map

What is a Mind Map?

A visual thinking tool by Tony Buzan (1970s). Write a central theme and radiate keywords outward as branches. It reveals the big picture of any topic at a glance.

What are the best mind mapping tools?

Popular digital tools include MindMeister, XMind, Miro, and Coggle. Analog mind maps with paper and colored pens are also highly effective. Choose based on your needs.

How do I combine Simple Memo with Mind Mapping?

Capture ideas on the go with Simple Memo, then structure them into a mind map at your desk. Separating capture from organization leverages the best of both approaches.
