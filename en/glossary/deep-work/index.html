What is Deep Work?

Deep work means focusing on a cognitively demanding task with distractions reduced. Cal Newport popularized the idea through his 2016 book Deep Work and earlier writing. Sitting for a long time or silencing notifications alone is not enough: first decide what you will think through or produce.

By AI Ataka

Examples here include developing an argument, evaluating design options or working through an unresolved problem. An email or meeting can also require substantial thought. Distinguish tasks by the thinking they need, rather than judging their value from the app or activity name.

Deep work concerns the kind of thinking; timeboxing sets the period for doing it. You can combine it with a timed method such as Pomodoro, but starting a timer does not by itself make the work deep.

Five steps for one focused work session

  1. Choose one output. If the task is reading, decide whether you will leave a comparison note or a list of questions.
  2. Set a start, finish and break. For example, try 30 minutes and adjust for the task and your energy; 30 minutes is not a research-established optimum.
  3. Open the needed material and working file first, and reduce unnecessary tabs and notifications. Agree how time-sensitive contact will be handled.
  4. Park an unrelated thought in one line and decide later whether to investigate it. When leaving the task, note where you stopped and the next action.
  5. At the end, review the output, reasons for interruption and next action. Choose one adjustment for next time, such as the duration or environment.

A brief break is not a failed session. Newport explains that deep work does not require maintaining peak concentration for hours without stopping. Take a break with a plan to return and adapt the session to the task.

What interruption research does and does not show

Sophie Leroy’s 2009 paper examined attention residue in two experiments: thoughts about one task can persist after switching to another. It does not establish that writing a note removes all cognitive load or guarantees a return to concentration within a fixed number of seconds.

Leroy (2009): attention residue during task transitions

Leroy and Glomb’s 2018 paper studied a brief plan for resuming interrupted work across four studies. Its main outcomes concerned attention and performance on the interrupting task. Do not reinterpret this as proof of improved performance after returning to the original task, or of the effectiveness of a particular notes app.

Frequently asked questions

What is deep work?
It means focusing on a cognitively demanding task with distractions reduced, an idea popularized by Cal Newport. It is not simply working long hours or silencing notifications.
How long should a session last?
There is no universal optimal duration prescribed here. Choose an output and a break, try the session, and adjust. Thirty minutes is an example starting point, not a scientific optimum.
Are all emails and meetings shallow work?
No. Some are routine coordination; others need difficult judgment or discussion. Classify the thinking required rather than the app name or meeting format.
What if an unrelated idea occurs?
Try parking one line and deciding when to review it. Researching the idea or processing your inbox immediately can expand the interruption, so separate capturing from handling it.
What should I record when interrupted?
Record where you stopped, what you established, what remains unresolved and the first action on resumption. The worksheet is a practical suggestion, not a guarantee of restored concentration or better performance.
Do I need a notes app?
No. Paper or your usual editor is sufficient. Simple Memo is an option for sending a short record to email or a configured destination, but operating an app is itself an interruption.
Capture a short note to review later
Review your sent note in your inbox or configured destination after the session. The free allowance is three sends per day.
