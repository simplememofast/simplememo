Public roadmap

We publish what we build next, before we decide

This app is not aiming to be a faster notepad. It is aiming at something else: you organise nothing, and the thought you had is already in the right place. The five things below are the route there. Tell us which one you want first.

What it does today

Capture from iPhone, Apple Watch, Siri (including through AirPods) and widgets — mostly without looking at a screen. What you capture arrives by email and is appended to your Obsidian vault.

Honestly: right now there is essentially one destination. Where a thought ends up barely branches. The five steps below are how that opens up.

The next five

The order is deliberate. We are not starting with "it files things for you". First it only suggests. Then it learns from the corrections you make. And before it learns anything, the rules you write yourself take priority.

1

Suggest a destination — without saving it there

When you finish writing, it offers "this looks like it belongs here". You decide. Nothing moves on its own.

Suggest only
2

Learn from your corrections

Every time you override a suggestion, that becomes the record it learns from. Without this, the suggestions never get better.

Learning
3

Write your own rules

"If it contains this word, send it here." An explicit rule you wrote beats a guess — and behaviour you can predict is faster than behaviour you have to check.

Rules
4

More ways in — share sheet, Action Button, Mac

An article in Safari, a photo, something shared from another app. And the same place to drop things while you are working on a Mac. Not a separate app per entry point.

Capture
5

More ways out — Reminders, Calendar, email and more

"Do this later" goes to Reminders, "next Tuesday" goes to Calendar. You still write the same way; the destination is what branches.

Destinations

What we are not building

Worth stating what is missing from the roadmap on purpose. These are not backlog items we never got to — they are decisions not to build.

  • A to-do manager. Reminders and dedicated to-do apps already do that better.
  • A calendar UI. Your events are easier to read in the calendar you already use.
  • A knowledge base or vault browser. That is Obsidian's job, or Notion's.
  • A read-later list. We are not adding another screen to read things in.

This app means to sit between the moment a thought appears and the place it finally lives. Owning the place it lives would put us in competition with it.

The survey

Every question is optional — answer one and send, that still reaches us. No account, no login. The options come from reading memo-app reviews across the Japanese App Store, so if none of them fit, the free-text box is the important one.

Q1. Which would you want first?

Pick up to three.

Q2. What is hardest about notes for you right now?

Pick one.

Q3. Which note apps do you use today?

As many as apply, including the ones you use alongside each other.

Q4. Anything the options missed

"I can't switch because X is missing" is the most useful thing you can write. Up to 1000 characters.

Q5. May we reply? (optional)

Only used to follow up on what you wrote, or to tell you when it ships. Leaving this blank is fine.

Email address

We keep your address only if you tick the box and fill it in, and only to reply. It does not subscribe you to anything. Handling follows our privacy policy.

Where these five came from

Two places. One is the direction this app has been heading since the start. The other is a sweep of memo-app reviews across the Japanese App Store, read in August 2026.

The clearest thing in those reviews: ratings drop for almost exactly two reasons, and neither is a missing feature. They are "will what I wrote survive?" and "how many steps before I can start writing?" The options in Q2 are the complaints that kept recurring, written out as they were said.

One caveat worth stating: that reading came from reviews surfaced by search, not from counting every review. It does not support a percentage, so there are no percentages on this page.
