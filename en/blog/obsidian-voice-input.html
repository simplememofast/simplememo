Voice Notes in Obsidian: 4 Ways to Dictate on iPhone

By AI ATAKA · Updated: October 4, 2026 · Reading time: ~6 minutes

Short answer. On iPhone, voice notes reach Obsidian by four routes: (1) Obsidian mobile + iOS keyboard dictation, (2) a dictation shortcut + the official Capture action or Obsidian URI, (3) a plugin that transcribes recordings, and (4) appending from Simple Memo to a vault you've set up. To try it without another app, use 1 or 2; to process recordings you already have, use 3. If you want one input screen and destination you reuse, 4 is also an option. Simple Memo's in-app dictation requires iOS 26 or later, microphone and speech permissions, a supported language, and a destination set up.

Live dictation vs. transcribing recordings

Obsidian ships a core Audio recorder plugin, but it stops at saving and embedding the audio file — there is no built-in speech-to-text. Dictating in the mobile app really means using the iOS keyboard microphone. So "voice notes in Obsidian" comes down to which input route you pair with which destination. Here are the four options.

Method 1: Obsidian mobile + iOS dictation (free, right now)

  1. Open a note in Obsidian mobile (a daily note works well as the default target).
  2. Tap the microphone on the keyboard.
  3. Speak — say "period" and "comma" for punctuation, or tidy it afterwards.

No extra tools needed. The weakness is friction: launch the app, wait for the vault, open a note, tap the mic. That is several taps, and mid-walk ideas often evaporate on the way. For seated writing sessions it is perfectly fine.

Method 2: Shortcuts (official Capture action or Obsidian URI)

Use the Shortcuts "Dictate Text" action to turn speech into text, then hand it to Obsidian. With the official Capture action, available since Obsidian mobile 1.11, the text is appended or prepended to a note without opening the Obsidian app. Set the destination inside the shortcut, and check that a short test lands in the note you intended. The same release also added Siri phrases such as "Capture to Obsidian". You can run a shortcut from a Home Screen icon, Back Tap, or the Action button.

Obsidian URI (obsidian://new) is a different hand-off: you assemble the vault, file and append parameters and encode them yourself. Don't mix up the two routes. For whichever you use, check its settings, what happens when dictation cuts out, and whether dictation needs a connection on your device and language.

Method 3: Whisper-style plugins (strong at transcribing recordings)

Community plugins can run recordings through Whisper-class speech recognition and turn them into notes. For meetings, lectures, or long voice memos, this setup suits turning longer recordings into text.

The cost is setup: API keys, usage fees, plugin configuration — and cloud-API variants do not work offline. Think "processing an audio archive," not "capturing ten quick thoughts a day."

Method 4: Append from Simple Memo to a vault you've set up (no plugin)

If the goal is "speak the moment an idea appears, and have it waiting in Obsidian," that is exactly what Simple Memo - for Obsidian is built for.

  • Turn on Auto-start voice input on launch (Settings → Voice Input & Sound) to start the in-app dictation when the app opens. It requires iOS 26 or later, a supported language, and microphone and speech recognition permission. Recognition runs on the device, but the first download of the language model may need a connection and some preparation time
  • When you send, the memo is appended directly, with a timestamp, to the Inbox note or daily note in the vault folder you granted access to. It does not wait for an email to arrive and then import it
  • Appending straight to the folder works offline as long as the target file is available on the iPhone. Email waits to send until you're back online, and syncing to your other devices is a separate step. You can also choose Obsidian-only mode (Premium)
  • About 0.4 seconds is measured from a warm launch to the moment you can type: the median of 5 runs on August 11. It is not the time to start recognition, deliver the email, or finish saving to the vault. The measurement conditions are published
  • Free: up to 3 sends a day. See pricing and plans for ongoing use

Which one should you pick?

  • Free and immediate → Method 1
  • One-tap trigger, enjoy tinkering → Method 2
  • Transcribing recordings → Method 3
  • Don't want to lose everyday ideas → Method 4

FAQ

Can Obsidian do voice input by itself?
On mobile you can use the iOS keyboard dictation inside any note. But Obsidian has no built-in transcription: the core Audio recorder plugin saves and embeds a recording without converting it to text.

How do I transcribe recordings into Obsidian?
Use a Whisper-style community plugin or an external transcription tool. That is the right setup for meetings and lectures. If you just want to speak and see text immediately, methods 1 or 4 are simpler.

Can I automate "speak, then it lands in Obsidian" without a plugin?
Yes. You can combine Shortcuts dictation with Obsidian's official Capture action. Simple Memo can do it too: on iOS 26 or later, set up voice auto-input and each sent memo is appended directly to the vault folder you granted access to. Either way, set the destination and check the first save; dictation depends on permissions, a supported language and whether the language model is ready.

Can I dictate to Obsidian from Apple Watch?
Yes. From the Simple Memo Watch app, the memo goes through the paired iPhone and is appended to the vault you've set up. The iPhone needs the integration set up and access to the destination. Email delivery needs a connection, and the vault can't be set up from the Watch alone. See our Apple Watch and Obsidian guide for the exact flow.

Method 4 is what Simple Memo - for Obsidian implements. For voice capture from your wrist, see Apple Watch × Obsidian.
