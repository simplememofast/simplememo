The 8 Best Note-to-Self Apps for iPhone (2026)
Compared by the Developer of One of Them

A note-to-self app should do exactly one thing well: get a thought out of your head and into a place you'll see it later — usually your own email inbox — in under three seconds. This guide compares eight apps against that standard — workflow, offline capture, encryption and price — with an editorial view of which one fits which user. It does not publish a cross-app speed ranking.

TL;DR

The closest match to the discontinued Captio workflow in 2026 is Simple Memo - for Obsidian (opens to the input screen, one-tap send, AES-GCM encryption on the device, offline Outbox, $0–$2.99/month; written by its developer). Drafts is the most extensible but takes more setup. Apple Notes is fine if you accept 4–6 taps per memo. EmailMe, Pigeon, MeMail, and Note to Self Mail occupy the middle ground. Captio itself left the App Store about two years before its cloud service ended on October 1, 2024, and is included here for historical context only.

What is a note-to-self app?

A note-to-self app is a single-purpose tool designed to capture a quick thought and route it somewhere you'll see it later — most commonly your own email inbox. The defining trait is speed: from tapping the icon to your idea being recorded, the entire interaction should take under three seconds.

Note-to-self apps are deliberately stripped down compared to general-purpose note apps like Apple Notes, Notion, or Obsidian. They have no folders, no formatting toolbars, no sync logic — just a text field and a send button. The category was popularized on iOS by Captio around 2010, and the design philosophy has been refined by a handful of indie apps since.

Why does this category exist at all when general-purpose note apps are everywhere? Because friction kills capture. If your tool requires you to choose a folder, pick a category, set a tag, or wait two seconds for the editor to load, the thought is gone. The note-to-self category exists precisely because that friction has a measurable cost on what survives from your brain to a place you can act on it.

CLOSEST TO CAPTIO — written by its developer
Simple Memo - for Obsidian (Captio-style Simple Memo)

"The closest match to the original Captio workflow, with on-device encryption and an offline Outbox that retries automatically."

Simple Memo - for Obsidian was built as a Captio alternative after Captio's cloud service ended in October 2024. The author had used Captio daily for 10 years before building this alternative from scratch.

The "open, type, send" workflow is the same, so the muscle memory from Captio carries over. Around it, Simple Memo adds AES-GCM encryption of the Outbox and send history on your device, an Outbox that queues notes written offline and retries when connectivity returns, and delivery through Cloudflare Workers and the Resend API (both third-party services) without OAuth access to your mailbox.

Pros: Opens to the input screen; one-tap send to a preset address. On-device AES-GCM encryption for the local Outbox and send history (email delivery itself is not end-to-end encrypted). Offline-first design with auto-retry. Free tier is genuinely usable (3 sends/day forever, no credit card).
Cons: iOS-only (Captio was iOS-only too, but that's a real limit for some). Text-only by design — if you need attachments, this isn't the right tool. Subscription pricing ($2.99/mo or $29.99/yr) for unlimited use. Disclosure: the author of this article built it.

Best for power users / automation
Drafts (by Agile Tortoise)

"Where text starts, then goes anywhere — but the configuration overhead is real."

Drafts is the most powerful app in this category. It treats every captured note as a "draft" that can then be sent through "actions" — send it by email, pass it to other apps and services, or run your own scripts. The flexibility is genuinely impressive. The cost is configuration overhead before the first send.

For users who want to capture text once and then route it through multiple workflows, Drafts is unmatched. For users who just want "type, tap send, done," it's overkill.

Pros: Action-based architecture is genuinely powerful. JavaScript actions enable arbitrary text processing. Strong macOS counterpart for cross-device workflows. Long-running developer (Agile Tortoise). Actions from the community Action Directory can be installed and run on the free version.
Cons: One-tap email to a preset address needs an email action with your address filled in, and per Drafts' documentation creating or editing actions — including pre-configuring recipients — is part of Drafts Pro ($1.99/month or $19.99/year), as are themes and workspaces. Steeper learning curve than any other app in this list.
One-time upgrade, no subscription
Pigeon (Email Yourself – Pigeon, by Ritchie & Mason)

"One-tap email to yourself, with photos, the share sheet and an Apple Watch app — and a one-time upgrade instead of a subscription."

Pigeon is a send-to-yourself app from the indie studio Ritchie & Mason, with App Store reviews dating back to 2020. Its listing describes one-tap email to yourself, sharing links from any app through the share sheet, photo attachments, Dark Mode, and apps for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. A recent update raised the number of saved addresses to five, and the developer says an offline mode has been added. Unlimited sending needs Pigeon Pro, a one-time purchase.

Pros: One-time Pro purchase instead of a subscription. Photo attachments and the share sheet. Apple Watch app. Up to five saved addresses.
Cons: The free version's sending limit is not stated on the listing. Widgets and on-device encryption are not verified here.

Voice notes, photos and widgets
EmailMe: InstaNotes (by Abhishek Chakraborty)

"Type a note, snap a photo or record a voice memo, and send it to your inbox."

EmailMe: InstaNotes sends typed notes, photos and voice memos (up to 10 minutes) to your inbox, and can be started from the share sheet, Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, or a Quick Action on the app icon. It can save up to 10 destination addresses. It does not send from your own address: the developer says it uses a fixed sender for speed. The first 3 notes are free; after that it needs InstaNotes Pro, a subscription. It is a different app from "EmailMe - Email yourself", a paid app by another developer.

Pros: Voice notes and photos. Widgets and Quick Actions. Up to 10 destinations. The listing states that the developer collects no data from the app.
Cons: Only 3 free notes before the subscription. Mail comes from the developer's sender, not your own address. iPhone only. Offline sending and on-device encryption are not verified here.

Checked against the US App Store listing on September 24, 2026.

Pay once, sends from your account
MeMail - Email yourself fast! (by Day One Design)

"A long-running, pay-once app that sends from your own mail account."

MeMail sends quick emails to an address you set once. Its App Store copyright line runs from 2010, and messages are sent from your own account. If you enter your mail account's settings, One-Tap Send skips the standard message screen; otherwise you send from the standard iOS mail screen, where the developer says attachments can be added. Its latest update, from July 2023, notes that Gmail is no longer supported for one-tap sending. It costs $1.99 up front.

Pros: One-time price. Sends from your own account. AutoSubject or a custom subject prefix, and an optional Cc address. The listing states that the developer collects no data from the app.
Cons: Last updated in July 2023. iPhone only. One-tap sending does not support Gmail. Offline sending and on-device encryption are not verified here.

FALLBACK — Already on your phone
Apple Notes

"The right answer for some users. The wrong tool for the job for others."

Apple Notes is a fantastic note-taking app, but it is not a note-to-self app. The distinction matters: capturing a thought and emailing it to yourself takes 4-6 taps through the share sheet. If your goal is to get a thought into your inbox before it disappears, Apple Notes adds friction at every step.

That said: if you don't email yourself often, if you're already organized in Apple Notes, and if you don't want one more app on your phone, Apple Notes is a perfectly defensible choice. It is built for a different job.

Pros: Already installed. Free. Excellent for long-form notes, formatting, attachments. iCloud sync across all Apple devices. Advanced Data Protection encryption is excellent if you opt in.
Cons: 4-6 taps per "send to self" — not the Captio-style workflow. Not designed for the "thought to inbox in seconds" use case.
HISTORICAL — Discontinued October 2024
Captio (by Tupil B.V.)

"Included for historical context. One of the apps that defined this category. No longer available."

Captio launched on the iOS App Store around 2010 as a one-time-purchase app that did exactly one thing: tap the icon, type a note, tap send, and the note arrives in your inbox. It was one of the apps that defined the category, and for over a decade it was many people's go-to tool for the "email yourself a thought" workflow.

In October 2024, Captio's cloud service ended, about two years after the app had left the App Store. The developer, Ben Lenarts, explained in an end-of-life notice on captio.co that he could no longer dedicate the resources needed to keep it stable and secure. The notice does not attribute the shutdown to Gmail, OAuth or an email API policy change.

You cannot install Captio in 2026, and because its cloud service ended on October 1, 2024, a copy still on an old device should not be relied on to send notes. If you used Captio, the notes it delivered are still in your email unless you deleted them (Captio was a sending tool, not a storage tool).

Sends through your own email account
Note to Self Mail (by Mathias Lipowski)

"Feature-rich, and it sends through your own email server or provider."

Note to Self Mail connects to your own email server or provider and sends notes in the background; it is not limited to Gmail. Its site lists multiple addresses, a custom sender, a customizable subject, share and action extensions, Siri (also on Apple Watch and CarPlay), text templates and an archive of sent notes. Attaching photos or files, sketches, audio notes and document scanning are part of the Pro version. The developer says the app uses no external services and does not store or share your data outside the app. Its latest App Store update is from October 2024.

Pros: Sends from your own email account. Multiple addresses and text templates. Archive of sent notes. Works with the email import of task apps such as Todoist, OmniFocus and Trello.
Cons: Setup means connecting your mail account, with provider-specific sign-in. Attachments and other media need Pro. Offline sending and on-device encryption are not verified here.

Which one should you use?

Three honest recommendations based on what matters most to you.

If you want the closest match to Captio

Use Simple Memo - for Obsidian. The workflow is the same open, type, send, and unsent notes wait in an encrypted on-device Outbox until they can be sent. The free plan lets you confirm the muscle memory transfers before paying anything. (Disclosure: built by the author.)

If you want maximum flexibility and don't mind configuration

Use Drafts. Once you set up your "Send to email" action and tweak it to your taste, Drafts is more powerful than any other app in this list. The trade-off is configuration time before the first send.

If you want zero new apps and rarely email yourself

Use Apple Notes with the share sheet. It takes more taps, but it's already on your phone, it's free, and if you only email yourself a few times a week, the friction doesn't matter as much.

FAQ

What is a note-to-self app?

A single-purpose iOS tool that lets you tap an icon, type a thought, and route it to your own email inbox in under three seconds. No folders, no formatting — just text and a send button. The category was popularized by Captio around 2010.

What is the best note-to-self app in 2026?

For pure "open, type, send" workflow, Simple Memo - for Obsidian (opens to the input screen, one-tap send, AES-GCM encryption on the device, offline Outbox; written by its developer). For extensibility, Drafts. For zero new apps, Apple Notes share sheet.

Why send notes to yourself by email instead of using a notes app?

Three reasons: (1) your inbox is the place you already check daily — notes that land there get processed; (2) email is a chronological searchable archive that survives any single app's shutdown (as Captio users learned); (3) email integrates with downstream tools (Inbox Zero, GTD, Zapier) without proprietary APIs.

Why was Captio discontinued?

The developer announced on captio.co that Captio's cloud service would end on October 1, 2024. According to that notice, the app had already left the App Store about two years earlier, and the developer could no longer dedicate the resources needed to keep it stable and secure. Full story →

Is Simple Memo - for Obsidian the official Captio successor?

No. Simple Memo - for Obsidian and YURIKA, K.K. have no affiliation, sponsorship, or partnership with Captio (Tupil B.V.) or Ben Lenarts. "Captio-style" describes the workflow design philosophy, not any official succession.

Do these apps work offline?

Simple Memo - for Obsidian keeps unsent notes in an AES-GCM encrypted on-device Outbox and retries automatically. Pigeon's developer says it has added an offline mode, and Captio supported temporary offline storage, according to its developer. Drafts keeps drafts in the app. For the other apps, offline sending is not verified here — check each app's current documentation.

Are these apps free?

Simple Memo - for Obsidian: 3 sends a day free; $2.99/month or $29.99/year for unlimited sends. Drafts: free, with Drafts Pro at $1.99/month or $19.99/year. Pigeon: free, with a one-time Pro purchase for unlimited emails. EmailMe: InstaNotes: 3 notes free, then a Pro subscription. MeMail: $1.99 up front. Note to Self Mail: free, with Pro features by in-app purchase. Apple Notes: built in. Captio was a one-time purchase and is no longer available. Prices were checked on the US App Store and developer sites on September 24, 2026.

What about privacy?

Simple Memo - for Obsidian encrypts its on-device Outbox and send history with AES-GCM; email delivery itself is not end-to-end encrypted. Apple Notes uses iCloud encryption (Advanced Data Protection if opted in). Drafts keeps drafts in the app and syncs them through iCloud. MeMail and Note to Self Mail send from your own email account; EmailMe: InstaNotes sends from the developer's own sender address. For sensitive content, look for on-device encryption + minimal-server architecture.
