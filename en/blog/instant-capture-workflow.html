Instant Capture Workflow
The Fastest Way to Save Ideas the Moment They Strike

Choosing a destination and category for every thought adds work before you start writing. This guide shows a capture workflow with one entry point, short notes and a later review, using an email inbox as a practical example.

By AI ATAKA · Published: March 16, 2026 · Updated: September 5, 2026

TL;DR

Instant capture means writing a short note before organizing it. Choose one entry point, keep the note focused and make time to review. With Simple Memo - for Obsidian, you type and send a note to your email. Launch time, typing time and email delivery time are separate; a 0.4-second launch does not mean the entire workflow completes in 0.4 seconds.

What Is Instant Capture?

"Instant capture" means recording a thought within seconds of its occurrence. The key is: don't organize it, don't categorize it, don't judge it. Just capture it. That's all.

David Allen's GTD (Getting Things Done) popularized this concept worldwide. His famous quote -- "Your mind is for having ideas, not holding them" -- captures the essence perfectly. When you try to keep ideas in your head, you consume working memory and lose the ability to focus on other thoughts.

Instant capture is the universal first step across all productivity systems. Inbox Zero starts with "collect everything into the inbox." Second Brain starts with "offload to your external brain." Zettelkasten starts with "write a fleeting note." Regardless of which method you follow, nothing starts until you capture the thought first.

Two sources of friction at Capture

Many people understand the importance of instant capture. But remarkably few actually make it a habit. There are two main reasons why.

Reason 1: Too many steps

Break your own workflow into opening the app, creating a note, typing and saving. Autosave and quick-entry options may remove some steps. Try your normal entry point and reduce actions before writing instead of assuming every note app takes the same amount of time.

Reason 2: Decision fatigue

"Which app should I put this in?" "Which Notion database?" "What tags should I use?" -- When you're forced to make these decisions before capturing, your brain gets tired and gives up on note-taking entirely. This is decision fatigue. When the destination is predetermined, this problem disappears.

The solution: Make your email inbox the single entry point

Send every memo to your email inbox. That's it. The "where should I save this?" decision vanishes. Since you check your inbox daily, you won't forget to process these notes later. This principle of "one inbox" is the key to making instant capture work.

3 Principles of Instant Capture

Principle 1: Zero-Decision Thinking

Never think about where to save. Predetermine one single destination -- your email inbox. When an idea strikes, your only thought process is: "open capture tool, write, send." By eliminating the decision step entirely, you minimize time-to-capture.

Principle 2: Reduce steps before writing

Check whether you are deciding categories or tags before writing. Note length and input method affect elapsed time, so three seconds is not a pass/fail rule. Try the same short sentence, such as “Send the Company A proposal,” and keep enough context to understand it later.

Principle 3: Single Inbox

When captured notes scatter across multiple locations, processing becomes a nightmare: "Where did I put that note?" When everything lands in one place (your email inbox), you simply review your inbox at the end of the day to process all notes. This is the same concept as GTD's trusted inbox.

A Day of Instant Capture

Here's what an instant capture workflow looks like across a real day.

7:30 AM
Commute inspiration -- On the train, you think: "Add that case study to next week's presentation." Open Simple Memo - for Obsidian, type "Presentation: add Company A case study to intro," send. Keep it to one short note. Works even in subway dead zones thanks to the outbox queue.
10:00 AM
Meeting insight -- A key point comes up in a meeting. Quick capture: "Check: Dept B budget approval flow." Meeting notes stay in the meeting tool; your personal action items go to your email via Simple Memo - for Obsidian.
12:30 PM
Lunch reading note -- A passage in the book you're reading catches your eye. "Reading note: 'Atomic Habits' p.142 -- importance of environment design." Instant capture. Transfer to your Notion reading database later.
3:00 PM
Task recall -- While deep in work, you suddenly remember: "I need to send that proposal to the client by Friday." Without breaking focus, send via Simple Memo - for Obsidian: "TODO: Send proposal to Client C (by Friday)." Process from inbox later.
7:00 PM
Evening idea -- "Should visit that shop for my wife's birthday gift this weekend." Instant capture. Personal and business memos all flow to the same inbox.
9:00 PM
Inbox Review -- End of day, check your inbox. Process today's 5 Simple Memo - for Obsidian captures one by one: some go to your task manager, some to Notion, some to tomorrow's calendar. Done in 15 minutes.

A one-line note you can process later

Use “subject | action or observation | date or source.” For example: “Company A | send proposal | by Friday” or “Reading | useful explanation in diagram | book title p.20.” These are sample entries. Do not invent a deadline; for reading notes, keep enough information to return to the source.

During review, move actions to your task manager, references to your notes, and delete what you no longer need. Archive processed emails so remaining ones indicate unfinished review. See meeting capture and the draft autosave guide for specific situations.

FAQ

What's the difference between instant capture and a regular note app?

Instant capture is a workflow, not an app category: record a short note before organizing it. You can use your existing notes app. Simple Memo provides an entry point into your email inbox, but steps and elapsed time depend on setup, text length and connectivity.

Where should I store instant-captured memos?

Your email inbox is ideal. Three reasons: (1) it's a place you check daily without fail, (2) it integrates naturally with major productivity methods like GTD and Inbox Zero, (3) you can later organize into your preferred tool like Notion or Obsidian. With Simple Memo - for Obsidian, memos automatically arrive in your email inbox.

Can I instant-capture while offline?

Yes, with Simple Memo - for Obsidian. When you write a memo and tap send while offline, it's saved to an AES-GCM encrypted outbox queue and auto-sent when connectivity returns. Works perfectly on subways and airplanes.
