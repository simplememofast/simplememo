Task Management

Email yourself for task capture
Review, deadlines and completion

Self-email can put a captured task in the inbox you already use. After sending, decide the next action, deadline and review time. This guide offers a small trial and client-specific setup for people who already review email regularly.

By AI ATAKA · Published: March 6, 2026 · Updated: September 26, 2026

Is sending yourself an email enough?

It captures the item; sending alone does not manage its deadline or completion. Combine capture with review, scheduling and a check that the work was actually done. Read/unread status describes reading, not the work’s progress.

When self-email fits

It is a practical trial for short personal tasks when you already review your inbox. Start by sending from your existing mail app; another app is optional. Simple Memo is one possible additional entry point for capture and sending.

For shared ownership, dependencies, recurring work or time estimates, consider a task manager or shared list. If your inbox already contains a large unprocessed backlog, decide when you will review it before adding more capture.

This is a suggested routine, not automatic task-state detection by the mail app. If you use labels such as “Next,” “Waiting” and “Reference,” define what belongs in each first.

Try one item through to completion

  1. Send yourself one short test and confirm arrival in the inbox, beyond any pending-send status.
  2. Inspect the received sender, recipient and subject. If useful, label messages using criteria that match the actual message.
  3. Write one next action in the new task’s title or details. For dated work, set its relevant schedule or deadline.
  4. Review this list at your usual email-check time, including waiting items and postponed work.
  5. Verify that the work is done and record completion in the system tracking it. Separately decide whether to keep or archive the email.

For recipient setup and arrival checks in Simple Memo, see the note-to-email guide. Keeping a pending send on the device does not deliver it to the inbox while offline.

Gmail: use Tasks and filter from the real message

In Gmail for iPhone/iPad, open an email, use the top-right menu and choose Add to Tasks. Check the task and its schedule in Google Tasks, then mark it complete after doing the work. This offers an entry point without retyping the email. Google: add a task from email

For a filter in desktop Gmail, start with Filter messages like these on an actual received message, inspect the matching results, then apply a label. A relay-delivered note can use a sender address different from your own even when you wrote it. Do not assume from:me will find it. Gmail filter instructions

If your chosen label is SelfTask, search with label:SelfTask. Combine it with is:starred only if stars have a defined role in your routine. Search operators

Snooze temporarily removes an email from the inbox and returns it at the chosen time. It does not complete the task or guarantee that a deadline will be met. Gmail snooze

Outlook: check flagged-mail and To Do conditions

Sign into To Do with the same Microsoft account used for email and enable Flagged email. Flagged Outlook messages can then be handled as tasks, with due dates, reminders and My Day options.

This applies to the primary mailbox of a Microsoft-hosted account. Shared folders and shared mailboxes are excluded. Initial population includes the 100 most recently flagged emails within the last 30 days, not your complete historical backlog. Microsoft: flagged email with To Do

Apple Mail: Remind Me is separate from task completion

In iPhone Mail, Apple’s guide describes opening a Primary message, using the Reply button, and choosing Remind Me with a date and time. It brings the message back toward the top of your inbox for review. Apple: check email in Mail

This is separate from creating a task with completion tracking in the Reminders app. For scheduled alerts and a completion list, check Reminders workflows and conditions. Inspect the actual device menu when your iOS version or display mode differs.

Archiving is not the same as completing work

Archiving in Gmail removes a message from the inbox while keeping it available in All Mail and search. A reply can bring it back. Archiving does not automatically complete work tracked in Google Tasks or To Do. Gmail archive

You can adopt a personal rule to archive completed work. But reference messages and work tracked elsewhere may also leave the inbox before completion. Check that next actions are clear and required work is finished, rather than using inbox emptiness as proof.

Frequently asked questions

Does emailing myself guarantee that I will not miss a task?
No. Confirm arrival, review at a chosen time, and schedule deadline-sensitive work. Sending alone does not establish those habits.
Does marking an email read put its task in progress?
Read status describes reading the email. Define next actions and completion separately using your labels or task system.
Why might from:me miss my captured notes in Gmail?
With a relay or similar service, the actual sender may differ from your own address. Inspect the delivered sender, recipient and subject and build search criteria from the real message.
Does an Outlook shared mailbox sync flagged mail into To Do?
Microsoft documents flagged-mail integration for the primary mailbox of a Microsoft-hosted account. Shared folders and shared mailboxes are excluded.
Does archiving finish the task?
Archiving removes the email from the inbox. Verify that the work is done and record completion separately in Tasks, To Do or the system tracking it.
Do I need to buy Simple Memo?
No. Start with self-email from your current mail app. If you want another capture entry point, check Simple Memo’s recipient setup, free allowance and pricing.
