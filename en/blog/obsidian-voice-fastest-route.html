Decide the definition of "fastest" first - the route consists of 3 sections

"Inserting into Obsidian by voice" passes through the same 3 section on any route when disassembled.

  1. Until you can speak — Open the app, open the note, press the microphone, call Siri......The section with the largest route difference
  2. talk - All routes are almost the same here (the length of speech depends on the content)
  3. Until it is written in the notebook in the storage room — Whether it is written on the spot, processed later, or another app will add it on the back

In other words, it is the sections 1 and 3 that determine the speed of the route. And the difference between the section 1 and 3 can be compared as a structure by counting "operations that cannot be saved" and "passing screens" without measuring the number of seconds. Readable from the table - Only the "does not open" route can operate 2 or less

Reading down the table, the dividing line is whether Obsidian opens. Routes that open Obsidian (①, ②', and ③) always include loading the vault and reaching the note in interval 1. Routes that do not (②, ④, and ⑤) write in the background, through a shortcut action or a local file append by the app, so interval 1 is reduced to simply starting input.

For the “launch” step in route ④, this site’s published measurement is about 0.4 seconds: the time from tapping the app icon until the 1 character can be entered, measured as the median of 5 warm starts in 2026-8. The procedure and raw data are on the measurement-method page. This measures interval 1 of route ④. Interval 1 of routes ①–③ and ⑤ was not measured using the same procedure, so this article compares the number of operations rather than presenting side-by-side timings.

Contents of 45 - Why can I "add it without opening it"

SimpleMemo, used in routes ④ and ⑤, relies on the fact that a Obsidian vault (Vault) is simply a folder. On iPhone, the app appends to that folder’s daily note through a local file write, so no plugin or launch of Obsidian is required. The same 1 line is also sent by email, except while “Save only to Obsidian” is enabled, so you can review it in your inbox even on days when you do not open Obsidian. Offline messages remain in the send queue and are sent automatically when connectivity returns. See the Obsidian integration overview for the complete mechanism and the dedicated daily-note append page for daily-note behavior.
