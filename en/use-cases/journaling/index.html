Email Journaling
for People Who Can't Keep a Diary

Started a journal and quit after three days? Downloaded a journaling app only to forget about it? The problem isn't willpower — it's friction. With Simple Memo, just send 1-3 lines to your email. In a few seconds, you're done. A searchable journal builds up in your inbox automatically, with little effort.

Why Journals Don't Stick

The reason journals don't stick isn't laziness. Most journaling methods set the bar too high. Sit down for 30 minutes, write beautiful prose, fill a full page — this can't survive once the initial enthusiasm fades.

Problem 1: Pressure to write a lot
The word "journal" creates pressure to write long entries. If you don't fill a page, it doesn't feel like you "did it properly." This pressure is a common reason people give up.
Problem 2: Can't build the habit of opening a dedicated app
You'll use a journaling app for the first week, but the moment you turn off notifications, you forget it exists. It's not a daily-use app, so it never becomes a habit.
Problem 3: Hard to search and review
Paper journals aren't searchable. App-based journals can only be read in that app. When you wonder "What was I doing this time last year?" there's no quick answer.

The Solution: One-Line Diary Sent to Email

Email journaling has one rule: "Send at least one line to yourself every day." Simple Memo makes this a few-second habit with its 0.4s launch (warm, iPhone 16e) and one-tap send. One line is enough. Entries accumulate in your email with timestamps, so you can search past entries instantly with Gmail or Outlook. Your daily email app becomes your journal.

How to Use It (5 Steps)

  1. Set a daily reminder — Use your iPhone's reminder or alarm app to set a notification at a fixed time, morning or evening. This becomes your journaling trigger.
  2. Launch the app — When the reminder fires, open Simple Memo. Cursor is ready in 0.4 seconds (warm launch, iPhone 16e).
  3. Write 1-3 lines about your day — "Highlight: got a compliment from a coworker" or "Grateful for: beautiful weather." One line is fine. No need for perfect prose.
  4. Send with one tap — Tap send once. Start the first line with [JOURNAL]; the first line becomes the subject, so you can filter and review just your journal entries later.
  5. Search and reflect via email — On weekends or month-end, search "[JOURNAL]" in your email. Past entries appear chronologically. Easily look back at "this time last year."

Real-World Scenario Examples

Scenario 1
Morning Intention Setting
Every morning at 7am, the reminder pings. While brewing coffee, launch Simple Memo: "Today's intention: finish presentation / hit the gym in the evening." Send. Your day has direction. At night, check email to review what you achieved.
[JOURNAL] Today's intention: finish presentation / gym / early bedtime
Scenario 2
Evening Gratitude Journal
Before bed, write "3 things I'm grateful for today." In bed, launch the app: "1. Coworker helped me 2. Great lunch 3. Kid's smile." Send. Done in seconds. Daily practice strengthens positive thinking over time.
[JOURNAL] Grateful: coworker's help / great lunch / kid's smile
Scenario 3
Mood Tracking
For mental health, you want to track your mood. During lunch break: "Today's mood: 7/10, focused in the morning, a bit tired in the afternoon." Memo and send. Review a month of emails to spot mood patterns.

Scenario 4
One-Line-a-Day Diary
"Sum up today in one sentence." While brushing your teeth at night: "A fulfilling day. New project kicked off." Send. A year later, read through 365 emails chronologically and see the story of your entire year unfold.

FAQ

What is email journaling?

Email journaling is a practice of sending short daily reflections to your own email. Instead of using a dedicated journaling app, your inbox becomes your journal — searchable, timestamped, and always accessible.

Can people who can't keep a journal stick with this?

Yes. The key is ultra-low friction. One line is enough, perfect prose isn't needed, and the whole process takes only seconds with a 0.4s launch (warm, iPhone 16e) and one-tap send. No journal to find, no pen to pick up. This low barrier is what makes the habit stick.

Can I search past journal entries?

Yes. Since entries accumulate in your email, you can use Gmail or Outlook search to find entries by keyword. Search "vacation" or "grateful" and see all related entries at a glance — a major advantage over paper journals.

Should I journal in the morning or at night?

Both work well. Morning is great for setting intentions and goals. Evening is ideal for reflection and gratitude. Start with one, and add the other when the habit feels natural.

Is my journal private?

Memos are sent only to the address you set, and no account is needed. Entries waiting to send and your send history are saved with AES-GCM encryption on your device. Memo bodies pass through Cloudflare and Resend at send time and are not persistently stored on our servers. Delivered entries stay in your own mailbox (and in your vault if you use the Obsidian integration).
