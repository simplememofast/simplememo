Email-to-Self Workflow Comparison

Simple Memo vs iOS Shortcuts
Which Is More Reliable for Email-to-Self?

Using iOS Shortcuts to create an "email to self" workflow is a popular hack. But it comes with setup friction, no retry queue of its own, offline behaviour that depends on Mail, and the risk of breaking with iOS updates. Simple Memo is a purpose-built app designed from the ground up for this exact workflow. Let's compare the DIY workaround with the dedicated tool.

By AI ATAKA · Published: March 7, 2026

What Is the iOS Shortcuts Email-to-Self Workflow?

iOS Shortcuts (formerly Workflow) is Apple's built-in automation app for iPhone. By creating a shortcut with a "Send Email" action, setting the recipient to your own address, and adding it to your home screen, you can approximate a "one-tap email to self" workflow. However, there are limitations: with Show Compose Sheet on, a compose screen opens before each send; the shortcut has no retry queue of its own (what happens offline depends on Mail and the account), and it keeps no send history — you check Mail's Sent and Outbox instead. Setting up the shortcut requires technical knowledge — adding actions, configuring variables, and testing the flow. After major iOS updates, it is worth re-checking that the shortcut still works. The key advantage is that it is free and uses Apple's native framework, but it is a DIY setup you build and maintain yourself.

Have Your Shortcuts Ever Broken?

If you have ever set up a "mail to self" shortcut on iOS, you may have experienced it breaking after a major iOS update. Major iOS updates can change how actions and permissions behave, so it is worth checking that a home-made shortcut still works as before. If it doesn't, you reconfigure actions, redefine variables, and test-send to verify it.

The root cause of this "breaking" problem is that Shortcuts is a DIY solution dependent on Apple's system APIs. Every time Apple changes email sending permissions or background execution rules, users must adapt manually. Simple Memo, as a dedicated app, implements email sending natively — iOS API changes are handled by the developer through app updates (Simple Memo supports iOS 16 and later).

Another overlooked issue is Shortcuts' "send confirmation" problem. When sending email via Shortcuts, the shortcut itself does not show whether the send completed — you check Mail's Sent and Outbox. What happens offline depends on Mail and the account; the shortcut has no retry logic of its own. Simple Memo uses an Outbox architecture that keeps memos on the device offline and sends them automatically when connectivity returns. Pending and failed sends are shown in the app, and arrival is confirmed in your inbox.

4 Differences Between Automation and a Dedicated App

  • 1 Setup: Install Only vs DIY Build
    Simple Memo is ready after install and email address entry. iOS Shortcuts requires creating a new shortcut, adding a "Send Email" action, configuring recipient and subject, and adding to home screen — a 5-10 minute DIY process requiring automation familiarity.
  • 2 Offline: Outbox Auto-Resend vs Mail's Own Handling
    Simple Memo saves offline memos in an AES-GCM encrypted Outbox and auto-resends on reconnect. A shortcut has no retry queue of its own; offline, it depends on how Mail handles the message. This matters in subways, flights, or spotty coverage.
  • 3 Send Experience: One Tap in the App vs the Send Email Action
    Simple Memo sends from its own screen with one tap. In Shortcuts, the Send Email action opens a compose screen when Show Compose Sheet is on; turning it off requests sending without it (permissions or a locked device can still interrupt the run).
  • 4 Long-Term Stability: Dedicated App vs iOS Update Breakage Risk
    Shortcuts can break after major iOS updates when action behavior changes — multiple cases reported. Simple Memo operates as an independent native app, unaffected by Shortcuts framework changes, providing stable operation across iOS versions.

FAQ

How do I set up an email-to-self shortcut?

Open Shortcuts app, tap "+", add "Send Email" action, set recipient to yourself, add "Ask for Input" before it, then add to Home Screen. Takes 5-10 minutes. With Simple Memo there is no shortcut to build — set and confirm your recipient address and you're ready.

My shortcut broke after an iOS update. Is Simple Memo more stable?

After a major iOS update, run a test send to confirm your shortcut still works as expected. Simple Memo doesn't depend on Shortcuts; support for new iOS versions comes through app updates.

Can I prevent the UI flash (Mail app briefly opening) with Shortcuts?

Turn off Show Compose Sheet in the Send Email action's options to request sending without the compose preview; permissions, account setup or a locked device can still interrupt the run. Simple Memo sends from its own screen with one tap.

Is there a reason to use both Shortcuts and Simple Memo?

Shortcuts excels at non-email automations (Wi-Fi toggling, app launching, file operations). But for "email memo to self" specifically, Simple Memo is the superior dedicated tool with offline support, send history, and encryption.
