Simple Memo vs Roam Research
Which Should You Choose?

The fundamental difference is how they handle thoughts. Roam Research networks knowledge with bidirectional links and block references ($15/month). Simple Memo sends short notes to your own email, with up to three sends per day on the free plan.

By AI ATAKA · Published: March 6, 2026 · Updated: September 5, 2026

This comparison is written by Simple Memo’s developer using published product information. We have not published a usage log or controlled speed comparison for these apps.

What is Roam Research?

Roam Research is a "note-taking tool for networked thought." It features bidirectional links, block references, graph views, and daily notes, structuring knowledge as a network rather than a tree. At $15/month ($165/year) with no free plan, it targets researchers, writers, and knowledge workers. Known for both its power and steep learning curve.

UI and Usability Differences

When you open Roam Research, you see your daily note with a sidebar listing pages and graph view navigation. You need to learn proprietary syntax: [[double brackets]] for bidirectional links and ((block references)) to embed text fragments elsewhere. Learning the linking and block-reference syntax takes practice; the time needed depends on prior experience and which features you use. Simple Memo's screen, by contrast, contains only a text field and a send button. It returns to the "origin" of note-taking -- you are typing within 0.4 seconds of opening the app.

The mobile experience also differs. Roam Research has an official iOS app, Roam Mobile, which opens the same graph as the web version on your phone. Its design, however, assumes you open your graph and write into the daily note, so the graph loads before you can type. Simple Memo's screen is a text field and a send button; you are typing 0.4 seconds after launch. For a 30-second capture on the move, that structural difference is the felt difference.

On pricing, Roam Research at $15/month ($165/year) is expensive for a personal tool. Judge the value by how often you reuse linked information and whether the graph helps with your research. For someone who takes notes only a few times a month, Simple Memo's free plan (3 sends/day) is a far more practical option. (Roam's pricing is taken from Roam Research's public site and may change; check there for current figures.)

3 Differences in Knowledge Structure

  • 1Bidirectional Link Thinking vs One-Way Email Delivery
    Roam Research networks knowledge with [[bidirectional links]] and block references. Simple Memo sends text one-way to email. If you need knowledge "connections," choose Roam. If you need thought "capture," choose Simple Memo.
  • 2$15/mo Research Tool vs $2.99/mo Capture Tool
    Roam is a $15/month advanced PKM tool for researchers and writers. Simple Memo Premium costs $2.99/month for instant capture. Prices may vary by region; see the App Store purchase screen. The question is whether you're investing in "structuring thoughts" or "catching thoughts."
  • 3An App That Opens a Graph vs an App That Is Only an Input Field
    Even on mobile, Roam opens your graph and writes into the daily note, so data loads before you type. Simple Memo is just an input field, launching in 0.4 seconds as a native iOS app. Whether you can capture a thought in a 30-second window while walking comes down to that structural difference.

Is Roam Research Worth the Price?

Roam Research costs $15/month ($165/year), placing it among the most expensive personal PKM tools available. For that price, you get bidirectional linking for networked thinking, block references for reusable text fragments, and a unique graph database architecture. These features deliver genuine value for researchers and writers who create dozens of links daily and rely on the graph view to see the big picture of their knowledge. But the reality for most users is different. For everyday capture needs — "I had an interesting idea," "grocery list," "keywords from a meeting" — you never touch bidirectional links or block references. Simple Memo - for Obsidian is purpose-built for instant capture; its Premium plan is $2.99/month (¥500/month in Japan). The optimal combination is using Roam for "structuring thought" and Simple Memo - for Obsidian for "catching thought." If capture is all you need, the $15/month investment is overkill.

Example: moving an emailed note into Roam

Suppose you capture “Customer interview question | ask about concerns before adoption.” During review, copy the email into a Roam daily note and link it to [[Customer interviews]]. This is a manual workflow example, not an automatic Simple Memo integration with a Roam graph.

Writing directly into your Roam daily note avoids copying if you already have it open. Email capture is an option when you want mobile notes to resurface in your regular inbox. Archive each email after transferring it so you can distinguish processed notes.

FAQ

Do I really need Roam Research's graph view?

A graph can help you examine relationships between topics and sources. There is no fixed note-count threshold for usefulness. Try linking your own material and see whether it reveals connections that search misses.

Is Roam's $15/month pricing justified?

For heavy users who create dozens of bidirectional links daily and use block references and queries, yes. If you only take notes a few times a month, Simple Memo's free plan (3 sends/day) offers vastly better value.

Can I replicate Roam's bidirectional links in Simple Memo?

No, Simple Memo has no bidirectional linking. Simple Memo is a "capture" tool; Roam is a "connect" tool. Use both together: capture with Simple Memo, then organize and link in Roam.

When are Roam's block references actually useful?

Block references help reuse a passage across multiple pages, such as research notes or related projects. Start with a small set of sources to learn how references behave.

Should I switch from Roam to Logseq or Simple Memo?

It depends on your goal. For linked knowledge, compare PKM tools and their migration formats. Simple Memo sends short notes to email; it does not replace a Roam graph or its block references.
